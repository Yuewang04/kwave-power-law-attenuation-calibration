\begin{abstract}
We audit power-law bone-like acoustic attenuation in k-Wave (MATLAB R2026a and CUDA v1.3) by separating algebraic reference-frequency parameter transfer from the intrinsic fractional-Laplacian solver dispersion physics near $y=1$. Under a standard interior-source 1-D benchmark (\texttt{source\_idx = 35}, \texttt{PML = 20}, 180~$\mu$s record, recording both $p_{\max}$ and $p_{\min}$), matching a linear literature coefficient at $f_0 = 0.5$~MHz reduces the $y=2$ calibration error from $-50.24\%$ to $-1.14\%$ ($R^2 = 0.999997$, $c_p = 3103.0$~m/s). Near $y=1$, default dispersion strictly obeys the Treeby--Cox analytical denominator $1 - (y+1)\alpha_0 c_0 \tan(\pi y/2)\omega^{y-1}$: at $y=1.01$, attenuation suffers a frequency-independent $-56.71\%$ to $-58.38\%$ deficit ($-57.58\%$ analytical at $0.5$~MHz) alongside a $+54.8\%$ superluminal phase-speed shift ($c_p = 4797.9$ vs.\ $3100$~m/s), matching a $+57\%$ peak-pressure excess in a 3-D control; at $y=0.98$, attenuation jumps by $+190.45\%$ ($+201.51\%$ analytical) while $c_p$ drops to $1818.7$~m/s. At $y=0.99$, negative effective stiffness $M(k_0) = -0.3505 < 0$ renders the wave equation Hadamard-elliptic, causing unconditional blow-up in both 1-D ($-9.44\times 10^{301}$~Pa across all frequencies and timesteps) and 3-D ($\sim 10^{30}$~Pa). Auditing k-Wave's built-in \texttt{fitPowerLawParams} reveals that it restores attenuation amplitude for $y \in [1.00, 1.10]$ ($-0.78\%$ to $-1.41\%$) while retaining $+54.7\%$ to $+103.7\%$ phase-speed distortion, and still diverges for $y_{\rm desired}=0.99$. Only $y=2$ (or $y=1$ with \texttt{no\_dispersion} in CPU MATLAB when phase dispersion is expendable) avoids both pitfalls.
\end{abstract}

\section*{TL;DR and v1.1.0 Scientific Erratum}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parameter transfer ($y=2$):} Direct copying of a linear ($y=1$) literature coefficient yields $-50.24\%$ bias at $0.5$~MHz; $\alpha_{\rm coeff}(y)=\alpha_{\rm lit}f_0^{1-y}$ ($f_0$ in MHz) reduces error to $-1.14\%$.
\item \textbf{Near-$y=1$ PDE mechanism:} Measured attenuation and phase speeds closely track exact fractional-Laplacian operator theory across both sides of $y=1$.
\item \textbf{v1.0.0 Erratum ($y=0.99$ and $y=1.05$):} Recording both $p_{\max}$ and $p_{\min}$ proves that 1-D at $y=0.99$ undergoes unconditional negative-going Hadamard blow-up ($-9.44\times 10^{301}$~Pa), identical to 3-D ($+10^{30}$~Pa); the earlier v1.0.0 ``$10^{-5}$~Pa collapse'' was an artifact of recording signed positive $p_{\max}$ alone. Similarly, sub-linear $\alpha_{\rm coeff}$ scaling at $y=1.05$ is not non-exponential decay ($R^2 \ge 0.999988$), but rather $\alpha_0$ appearing in the Treeby--Cox dispersion denominator.
\item \textbf{Full reproducibility:} Every 1-D dataset in \texttt{results/} is generated by \texttt{src/matlab/run\_all\_benchmarks.m} using standard interior-source placement (index 35) and $180~\mu$s duration.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analytical Theory: Parameter Transfer and Fractional-Laplacian Dispersion}
Matching $\alpha_{\rm kwave}(f)=\alpha_{\rm coeff}(y)f^y$ to $\alpha_{\rm lit}(f)=\alpha_{\rm lit,coeff}f$ at calibration frequency $f_0$ (in MHz) yields
\[
\alpha_{\rm coeff}(y)=\alpha_{\rm lit,coeff}f_0^{1-y},\qquad
\frac{\alpha_{\rm kwave}(f)}{\alpha_{\rm lit}(f)}=\left(\frac{f}{f_0}\right)^{y-1}.
\]
In k-Wave's first-order absorbing equation of state (\texttt{kspaceFirstOrder1D.m}),
\[
p = c_0^2 \left[\rho_x + \tau (-\nabla^2)^{\frac{y}{2}-1}\frac{\partial \rho_x}{\partial t} - \eta (-\nabla^2)^{\frac{y-1}{2}}\rho_x\right],
\qquad
\tau = -2\alpha_0 c_0^{y-1},\quad
\eta = 2\alpha_0 c_0^y \tan\!\left(\frac{\pi y}{2}\right),
\]
where $\alpha_0 = \mathrm{db2neper}(\alpha_{\rm coeff},y)$. In Fourier space, the dispersive stiffness multiplier is $M(k) = 1 - \eta k^{y-1}$. When $M(k) \le 0$ ($y < 1$ close to $1$), $c_{\rm eff}^2 = c_0^2 M(k) \le 0$, turning the hyperbolic PDE into an elliptic Hadamard ill-posed system whose modes grow exponentially at every timestep. For $M(k) > 0$, the fractional-Laplacian PDE delivers (Treeby \& Cox, 2014):
\[
\alpha_{\rm actual}(\omega) = \frac{\alpha_0 \omega^y}{1 - (y+1)\alpha_0 c_0 \tan\!\left(\frac{\pi y}{2}\right)\omega^{y-1}},
\qquad
c_p(\omega) \approx c_0 \sqrt{1 - \eta k_0^{y-1}}.
\]

\subsection*{Multi-frequency and timestep controls}
At $y=1.01$ ($M(k_0)=+2.35$), attenuation stays $-56.71\%$ to $-58.38\%$ across $0.2$--$1.0$~MHz (analytical: $-57.45\%$ to $-57.74\%$), with $c_p = 4689$--$4798$~m/s. At $y=0.98$ ($M(k_0)=+0.325$), attenuation reaches $+190.45\%$ ($R^2=0.999997$, analytical $+201.51\%$) with $c_p=1818.7$~m/s (analytical $1767.0$~m/s); timestep refinement (\texttt{dt}, \texttt{dt/2}, \texttt{dt/4}) keeps peak error at $+190.45\%, +190.18\%, +190.15\%$ and agrees with spectral FFT estimates ($+171.3\%$ to $+172.6\%$). At $y=0.99$ ($M(k_0)=-0.3505 < 0$), all seven frequencies ($5.9\times 10^{301}$ to $9.4\times 10^{301}$~Pa) and all timesteps (\texttt{dt}, \texttt{dt/2}, \texttt{dt/4}: $9.44\times 10^{301}, 5.99\times 10^{301}, 6.15\times 10^{301}$~Pa) diverge unconditionally.

\section{Conclusion and Reproduction}
Use $y=2$ with $\alpha_{\rm coeff}=\alpha_{\rm lit}f_0^{1-y}$ ($f_0$ in MHz) as the default portable configuration; use $y=1$ with \texttt{no\_dispersion} only on CPU MATLAB paths where phase dispersion is non-critical. All 1-D benchmarks reproduce via \texttt{src/matlab/run\_all\_benchmarks.m} (or \texttt{python src/run\_experiments.py}); figures and analytical self-tests run without MATLAB via \texttt{python src/simulate\_1d.py}, \texttt{python src/run\_experiments.py --plot-only}, and \texttt{python src/plot\_near\_y1\_frequency\_scan.py}.
